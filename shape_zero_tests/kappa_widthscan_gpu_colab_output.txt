WHAT THE PRINTED "HELD" DOES AND DOES NOT COVER
The prediction was that F agrees across beam widths at the same w/L. The
printout reports agreement within two combined error bars in all four groups.
That is a real test in only part of the scan:

  w/L = 1/4   SHARP (error bars +-0.03 to +-0.07). w = 3 and w = 4 give
              F = 1.32 +- 0.04 and 1.33 +- 0.07 -- agreeing with each other
              and with the model 2 - s = 1.35. w = 2 at L = 8 (1.23 +- 0.03)
              is the only precise point that stands apart: 1.8 combined error
              bars from w = 3, about 4 of its own error bars below the model.
  w/L = 1/8   PARTLY SHARP. w = 1.5 (2.01 +- 0.12) and w = 2 (2.22 +- 0.23)
              agree; w = 3 and w = 4 carry +-0.5 to +-0.65.
  w/L = 1/12, 1/16   NOT A TEST. Error bars of +-0.6 to +-3.1 would agree with
              almost any value. "Within 2" there means only "not resolved".

CONCLUSIONS
 1. Where it can be measured, F depends on the beam's proportions (w/L), not
    on the particular beam: the box-size mechanism is general across widths
    1.5-4, not tuned to w = 2.
 2. The 9% gap between F and 2 - s at w = 2, L = 8 is specific to the narrowest
    beam in the smallest box. Wider beams at the same proportions match the
    model. Coarse lattice resolution of that beam is a plausible reading; it
    is NOT established.
 3. The excess of F over 2 - s (10 of 14 points above) persists overall but is
    not uniform: at w/L = 1/4 the wider beams sit on the model.
 4. w = 3 kappa, correctly seeded, at four box sizes:
