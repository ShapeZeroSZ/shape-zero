# Target 1 — electromagnetism through dynamical u(1) links: hypotheses

*Committed before the scoping in `EM_SCOPE.md` and before any check script was written or run.
Each is marked in the scope report as HELD, FAILED or OPEN.*

**H1 — where the u(1) phase can live.** The existing links are velocity couplings,
c(W_n v_{n+1} − W_{n−1} v_{n−1}), and passivity forces each W to be symmetric (mission 2). A
local u(1) phase on such a link would be W = a·I + b·𝕁; its 𝕁 part is antisymmetric, so it does
work. **Passivity therefore forbids putting a compact u(1) phase on the existing velocity links.**
The phase must go on the elastic (position) coupling as a Peierls rotation,
c(U_n x_{n+1} + U_{n−1}ᵀ x_{n−1} − 2x_n) with U_n = exp(𝕁θ_n). That term derives from a potential,
so it is conservative. U_n commutes with 𝕁, so it is J-compatible. It is covariant under local
𝕁-rotations if θ transforms as a lattice gauge field.

**H2 — the dynamical ingredient.** θ_n becomes dynamical with a link kinetic term ½M θ̇² (the
electric energy) and a Wilson-type plaquette energy Λ Σ_P (1 − cos θ_P) (the magnetic energy).
Local gauge invariance then makes Gauss's law a conserved constraint, not an added equation.
**Constraints from the model's principles:**
- **Passivity** constrains the matter–link coupling (H1), not the plaquette form.
- **J-compatibility** requires the link group to commute with 𝕁. It selects U(1) ⊂ U(n)
  (generated by 𝕁 itself) for the n = 1 complex sector.
- **Neither principle selects the Wilson form over, say, Villain.** That choice is a selection,
  counted per the charter.

**H3 — parameters added.** The Hamiltonian adds three: link inertia M, plaquette stiffness Λ,
and the charge scale e in U = exp(𝕁eθ). One is a units choice (rescaling θ), leaving **two
dimensionless parameters**:
- the photon speed c_γ = √(Λ/M) relative to the matter wave speed √c;
- the coupling strength.

Requiring a single light cone (c_γ = √c) is a selection by a physical fact (Lorentz invariance).
It is counted, and it cannot then be claimed as a prediction. Net **+2 parameters**, one of them
selected.

**H4 — the long-wavelength limit.** In q = 3 spatial dimensions at weak coupling, compact
U(1) with this Hamiltonian gives:
- Maxwell's equations;
- a photon with two transverse polarisations and ω → c_γ|k|, with lattice dispersion
  ω² = c_γ² Σ_i 4 sin²(k_i/2);
- a Coulomb 1/r potential, i.e. an inverse-square static force.

**The dimension matters:**
- At q = 2 compact U(1) confines (Polyakov), so there is no inverse-square force.
- At q = 1 there is no transverse photon, and the static force is constant (a linear potential).

So target 1 is realisable only on the q = 3 base.

**H5 — the charge, and β̂.** The gauge charge is the Noether charge of the global 𝕁-rotation —
the "total phase charge" that J-compatibility conserves (MODEL_SPEC §3). For a wave e^{−iωt} its
density is ∝ ω|ψ|².
- **β̂ is NOT this charge's coupling.** Its Peierls angle, arctan(βω), grows with frequency, and
  in the continuum β is a uniform drift (MODEL_SPEC §1c). β couples to momentum and energy density,
  like a shift vector (g₀ᵢ), not to the u(1) current.
- So **the existing links' role is kinematic, a moving frame.** It belongs with target 2 (gravity),
  not target 1.
- κ, by contrast, is a rotating frame at μ = κ/2. With a dynamical U(1) that is exactly a uniform
  electrostatic potential A₀ = μ/e. **Larmor's theorem becomes a gauge transformation**, and a
  spatially varying κ acts as an electric field −∇κ/(2e).

**H6 — what exceeds the inputs.** Maxwell's equations, the photon and 1/r were used to select the
adaptation, so they count for nothing (charter rule 3). Candidate genuine conditions:
- the lattice photon dispersion shape beyond the one point that fixes c_γ, the analogue of the
  sin k condition;
- the κ ↔ A₀ identification (H5), a cross-sector relation;
- any relation between e and the existing parameters, if one exists.

**Expected net effect on the count:** parameters 3 → 5 and conditions ~6 → ~7–8. The count
probably gets **worse**, not better, unless the κ ↔ A₀ relation or an e–β̂ relation is real
and testable.
